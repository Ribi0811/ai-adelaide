# A clearer path from browsing to searching

## CarHire.global — website improvement example

A useful website improvement can be small and specific: remove something that gets in the customer's way at the moment they are ready to act.

On CarHire.global, the mobile chat launcher and its attention graphic could cover rental-search controls as a visitor scrolled. That creates competition between two useful features: searching for a car and asking for help.

The website update hides the floating chat controls while the mobile search section is on screen and on the booking page. Chat returns outside that area, remains available on desktop, and stays open if a visitor deliberately opens it. The Contact page remains another route to support.

**Before:** floating support controls could overlap the mobile search area.

**After:** the search controls have space while the visitor uses them, with support available elsewhere in the journey.

The 13 September release checks covered the mobile search/date picker, returning to Contact, opening and minimising chat, and desktop support. This is a documented usability improvement. It is not evidence of an increase in bookings, revenue or search rankings.

## What this means for a service business

The equivalent review for an Adelaide business starts with a real task: request a quote, choose a service or contact the business from a phone. We check whether the customer can complete that task without buttons, pop-ups or missing information getting in the way.

Any proposed work should name the problem, define the change and explain how it will be checked. Enquiry quality and sales outcomes need their own measurement after release.
